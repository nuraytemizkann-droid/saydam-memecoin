# Risk and transparency policy

## Public risk statement

$SAYDAM is a highly speculative, entertainment-first community token. It may lose most or all of its value. It does not represent equity, debt, ownership, dividends, yield, revenue share or a promise of future utility. Liquidity can be limited; large trades may face severe slippage. Smart-contract, wallet, bridge, exchange, regulatory and market risks remain even when the code is simple. Do not use borrowed money or money needed for living expenses. Verify the official contract address independently.

## Marketing rules

Never use: “guaranteed,” “safe investment,” “risk-free,” “next 100x,” “easy profit,” “floor price,” “will list,” “can’t lose,” or a countdown designed to imply scarcity that does not exist.

Performance screenshots require a timestamp, source and context. Paid relationships must be labelled clearly. No paid follower packages, engagement pods, undisclosed call channels, wash trading, volume bots, bundled wallets, coordinated pumps, hidden OTC discounts or private presales.

## Wallet disclosure

Maintain a public table containing the address, purpose, controller type, signer roles, allocation, vesting/lock terms and explorer link for every team-controlled wallet. Update the table before changing a wallet’s purpose.

Treasury reports should include opening balance, inflows, outflows, closing balance, transaction links, recipient purpose and any conflicts of interest. Publish monthly, and weekly for the first month.

## Corrections

Do not silently delete a material mistake. Post a correction with: what was wrong, the corrected fact, who is affected, the on-chain evidence, and what prevents recurrence. Pin critical security corrections until resolved.

## Incident response

1. Pause marketing; token transfers cannot be paused by the contract.
2. Post a short verified alert on both official channels and the website.
3. Preserve transaction hashes, messages and access logs.
4. Rotate compromised social sessions and multisig signers where possible.
5. Never promise reimbursement before the facts and legal options are known.
6. Publish a timeline and post-mortem.

## Anti-impersonation

Admins never DM first, request a seed phrase, ask users to “validate” a wallet or distribute surprise airdrop links. The site is the canonical source; X and Telegram must point back to the same domain. A new contract is never announced in only one channel.
